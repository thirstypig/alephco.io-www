FAQ

Common questions about compliance

The Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP) is an FDA rule (21 CFR part 1, subpart L) that applies to most US importers of food, with limited exemptions such as certain juice and seafood, food for research or personal consumption, and some alcoholic beverages. The FSVP importer must verify that its foreign suppliers produce food with the same level of public health protection as US requirements. FDA can refuse admission of food offered by an importer that is not in compliance (21 U.S.C. 381(a)(3)) and can issue warning letters.

A CPC is the certificate the Consumer Product Safety Act, as amended by the CPSIA, requires for children's products subject to a children's product safety rule. Based on testing by a CPSC-accepted third-party lab, the importer or domestic manufacturer certifies that the product complies with each applicable rule. For imported products, the certificate data must now be eFiled with the entry (16 CFR 1110.13).

Prop 65 is about exposures in California: a business with 10 or more employees may not knowingly and intentionally expose people in California to a listed chemical without a clear and reasonable warning (Cal. Health & Safety Code §§25249.6, 25249.11). If your products can reach California through distribution, e-commerce, or resale, check whether a warning is required. Retailers may also set their own requirements.

PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) are synthetic chemicals used in waterproof coatings, non-stick treatments, and stain-resistant finishes. Several US states — including California, Maine, and Minnesota — have enacted laws restricting PFAS in consumer products or requiring reporting. At the federal level, EPA's TSCA PFAS reporting rule (40 CFR part 705) requires a one-time report from companies that manufactured or imported PFAS, including PFAS in imported articles, from 2011 through 2022; the submission period opens no later than January 31, 2027. If your products use treated materials, check which of these apply.
