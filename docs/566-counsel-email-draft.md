**Subject:** Marketing copy review — compliance software positioning (2 docs, ~20 min read)

Hi [name],

We're about to market a feature and I'd like your read on the exact wording before any of it
goes live. Two documents attached; the second is the one I'd start with.

**What the software does, precisely.** It reads a document a customer uploads — a certificate,
a lab report — and reports which of the fields *typical for that document type* it could find
and which it could not. That's the whole mechanism. It doesn't assess whether the values are
correct, whether the document is authentic or current, or whether the customer is compliant
with anything. Our internal rule is that the software never renders a compliance verdict; that
call belongs to the customer's Qualified Individual, to their counsel, or to FDA/CPSC.

**The question I'd like answered first**, because it determines most of the rest:

> Our own rule says we will not describe a document as "compliant" — but it was written about
> documents the software *reads*. Roughly 26 live lines describe documents the software
> *generates*: a Children's Product Certificate, a Prop 65 warning label. For a certificate we
> validate against the seven content elements 16 CFR 1110.11(a) requires, and we can cite the
> paragraph. Is "this certificate contains the elements 1110.11(a) requires" a statement of
> **format** we may make, distinct from a compliance **verdict** we may not?

If format claims are acceptable, most of our existing copy survives with rewording. If they
aren't, roughly 26 published lines need replacing and our internal rule needs a scope note.

**Two things you should know before reviewing:**

1. Some of the copy in question is already published — it predates this review. The second

2. One part of the product currently reports a per-product status of "compliant" or
   "non-compliant" to customers. That contradicts our own positioning rule and we are removing
