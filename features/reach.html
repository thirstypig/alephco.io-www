REACH (EU) · Reg. (EC) 1907/2006

EU REACH duties for the articles you sell into the EU

For articles sold into the EU: the Article 33 and Article 7(2) duties for a Candidate List substance above 0.1% weight by weight, each row cited and naming whose duty it is.

What the rule asks

Three duties, each triggered by a Candidate List substance

Each applies only if an article contains a Candidate List substance above 0.1% weight by weight — measured in each component article, not the whole product (EU Court of Justice, C-106/14).

Information to recipients

A supplier of the article gives industrial or professional users and distributors enough information for safe use, including at least the substance’s name (Article 33(1)).

Information to consumers

On a consumer’s request, the supplier gives the same information free of charge, within 45 days (Article 33(2)).

Notification to ECHA

The EU producer or importer notifies ECHA when the substance is also present above one tonne a year, unless an exception applies (Article 7(2), (3), (6)).

What Aleph does

Cited rows that say whose duty it is

Rows are cited to Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006, as read from EUR-Lex’s consolidated text.

Answer the question first

Record whether the article contains a Candidate List substance above 0.1%, and whether it is over one tonne a year. A duty that does not apply says why; an unanswered one stays visible.

Whose duty it is

Each row names who holds the duty — whoever places the article on the EU market. For a company outside the EU, that is usually its EU importer, not the company itself.

Materials checked against the Candidate List

Each confirmed material is checked against the EU Candidate List by exact CAS number or name. A match shows a notice when REACH is on, or a suggestion to switch it on when it is off; it means the substance is listed, not how much is in the article.

Supplier declarations on file

Keep a supplier’s declaration of Candidate List substances and concentrations on the product. REACH does not require one, but it is one way to answer the question.

Exceptions recorded

Record when exposure can be excluded (with instructions supplied to the recipient) or the substance is already registered for that use.

On the same product

REACH sits next to the product’s US records — CPSIA, Prop 65, PFAS — so one record covers both markets.

What you still do yourself

Aleph covers these three duties only

  • Find out the concentrations, usually from your supplier or a lab, and answer the questions.
  • Agree with your EU importer who gives recipients and consumers the information and who notifies ECHA. Aleph does not notify ECHA.
  • Other REACH duties and other EU rules are not covered.
