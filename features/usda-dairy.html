USDA dairy · APHIS · 9 CFR 94

USDA dairy records for imported milk products

For imported milk products such as ice cream: answer where the milk came from, then keep the APHIS import permit, the government vet certificate or the organic import certificate on the product.

Get Started
What the rule asks

Milk products answer to USDA as well as FDA

USDA’s animal-health agency, APHIS, checks imported milk products for foot-and-mouth disease under 9 CFR part 94. Cheese, butter and butteroil are exempt (9 CFR 94.16(a)).

A permit, from an affected region

If any of the milk was produced in, or the product processed in or shipped from, a region not on APHIS’s list of regions free of foot-and-mouth disease, you need an APHIS import permit before you import (9 CFR 94.16(b)(3), 94.2(b)).

A vet certificate, from a free region

From a region on APHIS’s free list, each shipment needs a certificate endorsed by a full-time, salaried government veterinarian — except milk products from Canada (9 CFR 94.16(d)).

Egg, gelatin and organic

Egg and gelatin ingredients have APHIS rules of their own (9 CFR 94.6, 94.23), and each shipment sold as organic needs an NOP Import Certificate (7 CFR 205.273(d)).

What Aleph does

The right paper for where the milk came from

Each row on the product’s checklist is cited to 9 CFR part 94 or 7 CFR part 205, and asks the question that decides whether it applies.

Answer where the milk came from

Record whether the milk came from a region on APHIS’s free list and whether the product ships from Canada. The checklist then shows the permit row or the vet certificate row, and says why the other does not apply.

Permit with an expiry alert

Keep the APHIS import permit on the product with its expiry date, and Aleph alerts you before it lapses.

Shipment certificates on file

Keep a copy of the government vet certificate or the NOP Import Certificate that came with a shipment, on the product it covers.

Egg and gelatin, checked

If the product contains egg or gelatin, record what APHIS or your customs broker told you, with a note of who said so.

Customs broker on file

Keep your customs broker’s name, contact and filer code once on your company profile, and see it on each product that needs it.

Next to FSVP

An imported milk product usually needs FSVP records too; both live on the same product. See FSVP.

What you still do yourself

Aleph does not decide a country’s disease status

  • Check APHIS’s list of regions free of foot-and-mouth disease and answer the question for each product.
  • Apply to APHIS for a permit before the product ships, and have your exporter obtain the vet certificate for each shipment.
  • Your customs broker files the entry; Aleph does not file anything with USDA or CBP.
