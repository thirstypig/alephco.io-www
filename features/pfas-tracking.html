The Problem

PFAS regulation is a moving target

State by state, different laws

Maine, Minnesota, California — each state has its own PFAS rules, with different product categories, triggers, and dates. On top of those, EPA’s TSCA PFAS reporting rule (40 CFR part 705) reaches manufacturers and importers, including of articles.

Invisible chemicals

State PFAS laws name everyday categories — Minnesota’s bans cover cookware, carpets, cosmetics, and juvenile products; California’s covers textile articles. Your products may contain PFAS from manufacturing processes you don’t directly control.

Rules keep changing

PFAS rules are still being written and amended, at the state and federal level. Keeping up manually is a full-time job.

The Solution

One place for your PFAS record

Aleph tracks the PFAS requirements of California, Maine, and Minnesota today. Tell us what you sell and where — we show which of those requirements apply, each with its citation.

Per-state requirements, cited

For each product, see which tracked state PFAS requirements apply based on where it is sold — each with its statute citation and what you need on file. You decide how you meet each one and record it.

Supplier PFAS declarations

Email a supplier a link to a short PFAS declaration: does the item contain PFAS — yes, no, or unknown — and, if yes, in which components. Track who has answered. Your supplier answers through a secure link — no account, no password, nothing for them to set up.

Test reports on file

Upload lab test reports against each product, and find a testing lab from the PFAS page. Reading the results against each state’s rules stays your call.

Features

PFAS compliance, simplified

California, Maine, and Minnesota

A supplier who declares PFAS must name the component, and can give the substance and concentration in ppm — so you know which parts of your product are affected.

Documents in one record
