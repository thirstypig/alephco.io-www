What the rule asks

FSVP is a program you keep, not a form you file once

Most US food importers must have a Foreign Supplier Verification Program under FDA’s rule at 21 CFR part 1, subpart L (21 CFR 1.501 lists the exemptions).

Hazards, suppliers, verification

Unless an exemption applies, you document a hazard analysis for each food, evaluate and approve each foreign supplier, verify it, and take corrective action when a supplier falls short (21 CFR 1.504–1.508).

Ongoing, not one-off

You reevaluate a supplier promptly when you learn new information, and at least every three years (21 CFR 1.505(c)).

Records on request

You make your FSVP records available promptly when FDA asks, and keep most of them at least two years (21 CFR 1.510(b), (c)). Food from an importer who isn’t complying is subject to refusal of admission (21 CFR 1.514).

What Aleph does

Your FSVP records, built and signed in one place

Signed FSVP records

Fill in the qualified individual record, the hazard analysis, the supplier evaluation and approval, verification activities and corrective actions in Aleph; each signed record carries the signer and date, and your written procedures are drafted from the verification decisions you record. Supplier profiles

Record each foreign supplier with its contact and facility details, your evaluation and approval, and its verification history — linked to the products it makes.

Documents on the record

Upload supplier agreements, audit reports and certificates of analysis, and file each one against the supplier and product it covers.

Verification due dates

Upcoming and overdue verification activities show in your compliance alerts, and the checklist cites the duty to reevaluate each supplier (21 CFR 1.505(c)).

Inspection package

Download the FSVP records for the products you choose as one organized ZIP, on every plan. On paid plans you can also send it from Aleph to an FDA agent, lawyer or broker as a secure link that lasts 3, 14 or 30 days, and withdraw it at any time.

Broker sheet and action log

A per-product broker sheet (PDF or CSV) carries the FDA product code and the manufacturer’s FDA registration number you record. Uploads, signings and changes are timestamped in your action log.

What you still do yourself

Aleph keeps the record; the decisions are yours

  • Decide whether an exemption applies, which hazards need a control, and which verification activities to run — with your qualified individual.
  • Run the audits, sampling and testing, and sign each record yourself.
  • Give FDA the records when it asks. Aleph does not submit anything to FDA.
