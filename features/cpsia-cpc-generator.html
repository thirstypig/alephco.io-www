What the rule asks

Imported products a CPSC rule covers need a certificate

For a product made outside the US, the importer is the certifier who must issue the certificate (16 CFR 1110.7(a)).

CPC or GCC

A children’s product subject to a children’s product safety rule needs a Children’s Product Certificate based on third-party testing by a CPSC-accepted lab. Other products a CPSC rule covers need a General Certificate of Conformity. An imported product not accompanied by a required certificate shall be refused admission (15 U.S.C. 2066(a)).

Seven elements, eFiled at entry

A certificate carries seven elements: the product, each rule it certifies to, the certifier, the records contact, the date and place of manufacture, the date and place of testing, and the attestation (16 CFR 1110.11(a)). Since July 8, 2026, imported products’ certificate data must be eFiled at entry (16 CFR 1110.13).

Testing and records

Children’s products need periodic testing — at least once a year, or every two or three years under 16 CFR 1107.21(c) or (d) — and you keep certificates and the records behind them at least five years (16 CFR 1110.17).

What Aleph does

Your certificate, built from your records

CPC and GCC share one certificate form in Aleph: a CPC for a children’s product, a GCC for any other product a CPSC rule covers.

Build, attest, print

Aleph builds the certificate from the product’s records — you choose the rules it certifies to and attest to it as the certifier — and prints it as a PDF. Or upload a certificate you already issued.

CPSC’s accepted-lab list

Pick your lab from CPSC’s list of accepted testing laboratories, with a link to CPSC’s own search to check a lab’s current status. Anyone can search the same list in our testing lab directory. Confirming that a lab is accepted for your tests stays your call.

Lab reports on the product

Upload the test reports and file each one against the products it covers. Aleph reads the document and suggests its type and dates for you to confirm.

A cited records checklist

Each product’s checklist lists the records the rules require you to keep — the certificate, the test records behind it, periodic testing and the tracking label — each with its citation.

Expiry and re-test alerts

Give a lab report or certificate an expiry or re-test date and Aleph warns you ahead of it — first 90 days before by default (or 30, 60 or 180), then at 30 and 7 days, in the app and by email.

For your broker

Download the certificate data as a CPSC Registry CSV, or a broker sheet with your Certifier ID, Product ID and Version ID for each product.

CPSC Product Registry

Send it to your CPSC Product Registry

For eFiling, your customs broker can point to a certificate that is already in your CPSC Product Registry instead of re-entering it. Aleph sends your attested CPC or GCC there through your own Registry account, when you click.

1

Connect your own Registry

Create an API token in your CPSC Product Registry account and save it in Aleph. Aleph keeps it encrypted, and nothing goes to CPSC until you click Send.

2

Send an attested certificate in one click

Aleph shows CPSC’s answer — imported, or CPSC’s own error — and the certificate’s status in your Registry, such as Awaiting Certification or Complete, and records the day it was imported.

3

Certify it in your Registry

Certifying is your company’s own statement to CPSC, so it stays your step: the certificate arrives Awaiting Certification unless you choose otherwise, and you certify it in your Registry.

4

Hand your broker three IDs

At entry, your broker references the certificate by your Certifier ID, Product ID and Version ID. Aleph’s broker sheet lists all three, with the Registry status Aleph last read.

No duplicate manufacturers or labs

A manufacturer, lab or records contact Aleph already sent to your Registry is answered “already in your Registry”, so it isn’t created a second time.

What you still do yourself

Certifying is your company’s statement

  • Decide which rules apply to the product and which it certifies to, with your lab, and attest to the certificate.
  • Certify the certificate in your CPSC Product Registry, and have your customs broker eFile or reference it at entry. Aleph does not file the entry.
  • For a certificate that cites ASTM F963, pick the F963 sections CPSC lists (for example 4.6 Small Objects) in the certificate’s rules; you decide which sections apply.
