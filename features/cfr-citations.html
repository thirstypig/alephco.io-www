CFR Citations

Every requirement tells you which paragraph it came from

Aleph doesn’t hand you a score. It hands you a list of requirements, each one naming the exact provision behind it — down to the paragraph, like 16 CFR 1110.11(a)(1) — so you can read the rule yourself and decide whether we got it right.

The Problem

A verdict you can’t check is a verdict you can’t defend

“Compliant” isn’t an answer

A green tick tells you what some software concluded. It doesn’t tell you what rule it applied, whether that rule is the current one, or whether it applies to your product at all.

You are the one who has to answer

When an inspector, a customer’s quality team or your own counsel asks why you did something, “the software said so” is not a position. The citation is what turns a checklist into a defence.

Part-level is too coarse to use

Being pointed at “16 CFR Part 1110” and told something applies still leaves you reading the whole part to find the sentence. The paragraph is where the obligation actually sits.

What We Do

58 cited elements across six regimes

Each requirement in Aleph carries its own citation. A requirement cannot exist in the system without one.

Down to the paragraph

Not “Part 1110” but 16 CFR 1110.11(a)(1). A part, a section, and the specific paragraph — the level at which you can actually read the sentence that creates the duty.

Regulations and statutes

Citations reach both the Code of Federal Regulations — 16 CFR for consumer products, 21 CFR for food and cosmetics — and the underlying statutes at 15 and 21 U.S.C. where the duty starts in the law itself.

A versioned rule set

The requirement list carries a version stamp, so a checklist you generated last quarter can be told apart from one generated today — and you can see which reading of the rules produced it.

Coverage

Which regimes carry cited requirements

FSVP

Foreign Supplier Verification Programs, cited through 21 CFR Part 1 — the subpart L sections covering hazard analysis, supplier evaluation, verification activities and records.

CPSIA, CPC and GCC

Children’s Product Certificates and General Certificates of Conformity, cited through 16 CFR 1107 and 1110, plus the certification duty at 15 U.S.C. 2063(a)(1).

Prop 65, PFAS and MoCRA

California’s warning requirements, state PFAS disclosure duties, and cosmetics obligations under MoCRA at 21 U.S.C. — each requirement carrying the provision it derives from.

EU REACH is tracked in Aleph, but its requirement list is not yet specified to this standard — so it is not included in the count above.

Being Clear

A citation is not legal advice

Naming the paragraph tells you where a requirement comes from and lets you check it against the regulation yourself. It is not a legal opinion, and Aleph does not tell you that you are compliant — it keeps the record of what you did, cited, so that you or your counsel can make that judgement. Regulations change; the alerts we send when a watched rule is amended exist for exactly that reason.
