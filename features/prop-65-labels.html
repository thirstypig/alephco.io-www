What the rule asks

A clear and reasonable warning, or a reason you need none

A business with 10 or more employees may not knowingly and intentionally expose people in California to a listed chemical without a clear and reasonable warning (Cal. Health & Safety Code 25249.6, 25249.11).

Hundreds of listed chemicals

OEHHA’s list names hundreds of chemicals known to the state to cause cancer or reproductive harm. Many have a No Significant Risk Level or Maximum Allowable Dose Level; many do not.

Short-form warnings changed

Amendments operative January 1, 2025, require new short-form warnings to name at least one listed chemical; products manufactured and labeled before January 1, 2028, may still use the earlier text (27 CCR 25603). Food has its own warning format.

Lawsuit risk

Besides public prosecutors, private enforcers can sue in the public interest after a 60-day notice, and civil penalties run up to $2,500 per day for each violation.

What Aleph does

Record your decision. Generate the label.

Whether a warning is required is your determination — Aleph records it with the product.

OEHHA’s chemical list

Search the Prop 65 list by chemical name or CAS number, with each entry’s listing type (cancer, reproductive, or both). The list is imported from OEHHA’s published file.

Your warning decision

Record whether each product needs a warning, which chemicals, the warning type and exposure routes, and attach your lab report — or record that no warning is needed, and why.

Short-form and long-form labels

Generate either label as a PDF with the warning triangle and the P65Warnings.ca.gov reference; the long form names the chemicals you select. A food product’s label uses the food warning format instead.

Materials checked against the list

Each confirmed ingredient or material is checked against the Prop 65 list by exact CAS number or name. A match means the chemical is on the list, not how much of it is in the product.

Safe harbor determination, recorded

Record whether you consider your warning safe harbor compliant, from your own review of OEHHA’s rules. Aleph does not make that call; it keeps your answer with the product.

Audit trail

Each saved assessment and each generated label is recorded in your action log, so you can show what you decided and when.

What you still do yourself

The exposure call stays with you

  • Decide whether a product causes an exposure that needs a warning, with your lab or advisor.
  • Check the generated label against 27 CCR 25603 for your products, and put it on the product, its packaging or your product page.
  • Answer any 60-day notice. Aleph does not give legal advice.
