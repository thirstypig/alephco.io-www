⚠️ **Read this as a prompt to check, not as a source of dates to act on.** Everything below
comes from a rule already in force, verified against its own text — but deadlines move, and
this article was written before the year it describes. Follow each link before you act.

Dates verified on **6 September 2026**.

## January

**1 January — New Mexico prohibitions and reporting begin.** Cookware, food packaging,
dental floss, juvenile products and firefighting foam containing intentionally added PFAS
may no longer be sold. Reporting and labelling obligations start the same day
([20.13.2 NMAC](https://www.env.nm.gov/opf/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2026/04/EXHIBIT-A-20.13.2-NMAC-FINAL.pdf)).
Note the labelling rule reaches products **manufactured after** 1 January 2027.

**1 January — Washington's next PFAS restriction tranche.** Apparel and accessories,
footwear, and gear for recreation and travel, among others, may not be manufactured, sold
or distributed with intentionally added PFAS. The restriction does not apply to product
**manufactured before** that date
([WAC 173-337](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=173-337)).

**8 January — CPSC eFiling reaches foreign trade zones.** The main rule took effect 8 July
2026; entries from an FTZ for consumption or warehousing follow now
([final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/08/2024-30826/certificates-of-compliance)).
If you use an FTZ, this is the one to check with your broker.

**31 January — Washington PFAS reporting.** The first notification is due, and annually
thereafter by 31 January.

## February

**1 February — Minnesota PFAS reporting update.** Annual updates fall on 1 February after
the initial report ([§116.943](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/116.943)). See
[PFAS rules for importers](/blog/pfas-rules-for-importers.html).

## Rolling through the year

**MoCRA facility renewals.** Biennial, and dated from **your** initial registration rather
than a common date — so they land whenever they land. See
[MoCRA for cosmetics importers](/blog/mocra-for-cosmetics-importers-registration-and-listing.html).

**FSVP supplier reevaluations.** At least every three years under §1.505(c). For anyone who
built their programme in 2024, this is the year it comes round.

**Children's product periodic testing.** Annually, or on your longer interval if the
supporting plan exists ([16 CFR §1107.21](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-1107)).

## ⚠️ Two things widely quoted as 2027 that are not

**Connecticut's PFAS prohibition is 1 January 2028**, not 2027. What began in 2026 was the
notification and labelling obligation. See
[which states require what](/blog/pfas-rules-for-importers.html).

**The Prop 65 short-form warning changeover is 1 January 2028** — and even then it applies
to product **manufactured and labelled** from that date, with unlimited sell-through of
older stock ([27 CCR §25603(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/27-CCR-25603)).
See [what changed](/blog/short-form-warnings-what-changed.html).

Both get pulled forward in summaries. Acting a year early on the Prop 65 change in
particular means scrapping labels you were entitled to keep using.

## The pattern worth noticing

Look at the wording on the PFAS restrictions above: New Mexico's labelling reaches products
*manufactured after*, Washington's restriction exempts products *manufactured before*, and
California's short-form transition keys on *manufactured and labelled prior to*.

**Almost none of these key on the date of sale.** They key on when the product was made.
Which means the operative question in your catalogue is not "what are we selling in 2027?"
but "what will we still be *producing* after each of these dates, and to what
specification?" Existing stock is usually safe; the next purchase order usually is not.

That distinction decides whether these deadlines cost you a label change or an inventory
write-off, and it is the single most common thing to get backwards.
