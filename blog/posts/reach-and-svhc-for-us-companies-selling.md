The most useful thing to understand about REACH, if you are a US company, is that it
probably does not apply to you directly. It applies with force to your customer, and that is
why their questionnaire arrives with a deadline and a tone.

Quoted from the consolidated Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006, checked on **6 September 2026**.

## The duty follows EU establishment

REACH obligations attach to entities established in the EU. A US manufacturer selling to an
EU distributor is not the importer — the distributor is, and the registration and
notification duties are theirs.

Article 8 provides the one route by which a non-EU company takes the duty on deliberately:

> A natural or legal person established outside the Community who manufactures a substance
> on its own, in mixtures or in articles, formulates a mixture or produces an article that is
> imported into the Community may by mutual agreement appoint a natural or legal person
> established in the Community to fulfil, as his only representative, the obligations on
> importers under this Title.

An **Only Representative** is something you choose, usually to relieve your EU customers of
duties they would rather not carry. It is a commercial decision with a legal form, not a
default obligation.

## What your customer is actually obliged to do

**Article 33(1)** — the one behind almost every questionnaire you receive:

> Any supplier of an article containing a substance meeting the criteria in Article 57 and
> identified in accordance with Article 59(1) in a concentration above 0,1 % weight by weight
> (w/w) shall provide the recipient of the article with sufficient information, available to
> the supplier, to allow safe use of the article including, as a minimum, **the name of that
> substance**.

**Article 33(2)** adds the consumer-facing version, with a clock:

> On request by a consumer … The relevant information shall be provided, **free of charge,
> within 45 days** of receipt of the request.

Your customer cannot answer either without data from you. That is the entire mechanism by
which REACH reaches a US supplier: contractually, through someone who has 45 days.

## The notification threshold is different, and has two limbs

Article 7(2) requires notification to the Agency where a Candidate List substance is present
in articles and **both**:

> (a) the substance is present in those articles in quantities totalling over **one tonne per
> producer or importer per year**; (b) the substance is present in those articles above a
> concentration of **0,1 % weight by weight (w/w)**.

Both limbs, not either. And Article 7(3) carves out cases where the producer or importer can
exclude exposure to humans or the environment during normal or reasonably foreseeable
conditions of use **including disposal** — in which case they must instead supply appropriate
instructions to the recipient.

So a substance above 0.1% does not automatically trigger notification. Volume matters, and so
does whether exposure can be excluded.

## Two things worth getting right

**0.1% is a concentration in the article, and "the article" is the argued question.** The
threshold in Articles 7 and 33 is expressed against the article containing the substance. How
that applies to an assembled product made of many components is the point on which answers
most often differ — ask your customer which basis they are working to before you answer, and
record the basis alongside your answer.

**The Candidate List is a moving list.** It is maintained by ECHA under Article 59 and
updated periodically. Any answer you give is an answer as at a date, against a version of the
list — which is why an undated declaration is close to worthless here, exactly as it is for
[PFAS](/blog/getting-a-pfas-declaration-out-of-an.html). Check the current list at
[ECHA](https://echa.europa.eu/candidate-list-table) rather than working from a copy.

## What to do as the US supplier

1. **Ask which duty they are discharging.** Article 33 communication and Article 7(2)
   notification need different information. The second needs tonnage.
2. **Answer per article, with the basis stated.** Component or assembled product — say which.
3. **Give the substance name and CAS number.** Article 33 sets the substance name as the
   minimum, and a name without a CAS number creates a second round of email.
4. **Date every answer, and name the list version** you checked it against.
5. **Consider an Only Representative** if EU sales are strategic. It moves a burden your
   customers feel, and they know it.
6. **Reuse the work.** The data behind a REACH answer is the same data behind
   [PFAS reporting](/blog/pfas-rules-for-importers.html) and
   [Prop 65](/blog/prop-65-for-importers-who-it-applies.html) — which substances, at what
   concentration, doing what job. Collect it once.
