At least seven states now require the "producer" of packaged goods to help pay for what
happens to that packaging after the customer throws it out: Maine, Oregon, Colorado,
California, Minnesota, Maryland and Washington. Treat them as one law with seven names and
you'll get at least one deadline wrong. They run on different schedules, they don't all
define "producer" the same way, and one of them has no operating program at all yet.

Checked against each state's own statute, agency page or court docket on **10 September 2026**.
These programs move fast. If you're reading this much later, check the links rather than
the text.

## Who counts as the "producer"

Most of these laws hand the obligation down a ladder: the manufacturer selling under its
own brand, then a licensee, then the brand owner. If none of those is in the United States,
it lands on the importer. That's the structure in
[Colorado](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2022a_1355_signed.pdf) ("the person
that imports the product… into the United States"),
[Maine](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/38/title38sec2146.html) and
[Minnesota](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/115A.1441) (both say "importer of
record into the United States"), and
[Oregon](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/recycling/Documents/RMAProducerObligationsSummary.pdf):
"If no such entity is located in the U.S., then the obligated producer is the entity that
imports the packaged item into the country."

**California is the exception.**
[PRC §42041](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PRC&sectionNum=42041)
asks whether a manufacturer or brand owner is *in the state*, not in the US. Its last
resort isn't the importer, either. It's "the person who sells, offers for sale, or
distributes the product… in or into the state."

Online sales add a second producer. The packaging a product ships in belongs to whoever
packs and ships the order. The product's own packaging still belongs to the brand-side
producer. Oregon's DEQ gives the example directly: "the obligated producer of the
packaging used for shipping (e.g., Amazon) must notify the producer of the sold covered
product (e.g., L'Oreal)." Colorado, Maine and Minnesota split remote sales the same way.

If you import private-label or unbranded goods with no US brand owner behind them, read
that twice. In at least four of these states, the law is pointing at you.

Small businesses can be exempt, but the thresholds and the scope differ:

| State | Small-producer exemption, as the state words it |
|---|---|
| Oregon | Under $5 million gross global revenue, or under 1 metric ton sold into Oregon ([DEQ summary, Feb 2025](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/recycling/Documents/RMAProducerObligationsSummary.pdf)) |
| California | Under $1 million gross annual sales, and you have to [apply for it](https://calrecycle.ca.gov/packaging/packaging-epr/exemptionsexclusions/) |
| Maine | Under $2 million gross revenue, or under 1 ton of packaging ([38 M.R.S. §2146(2)](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/38/title38sec2146.html)) |
| Minnesota | Under 1 ton introduced, or under $2 million global gross revenue ([§115A.1441](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/115A.1441)) |
| Colorado | A revenue ceiling [CDPHE adjusts for inflation each year](https://cdphe.colorado.gov/hm/epr-program) |

California's is narrower than it looks. It covers "most of the law's reporting and fee
requirements", and CalRecycle says exempt producers still
"need to plan for long-term compliance to ensure all packaging they sell is recyclable or
compostable by 2032."

## Where each state stands

**Oregon** (Recycling Modernization Act). DEQ's summary is plain: "By July 1, 2025,
individual producers must: Register with and be a member of a producer responsibility
organization (PRO)" and pay it an annual fee. The 25 largest producers also have to
"perform and disclose evaluations of the life cycle impacts of at least one percent of
their covered products" every two years. The PRO is Circular Action Alliance, whose
[approved plan](https://www.oregon.gov/deq/recycling/pages/modernizing-oregons-recycling-system.aspx)
was last amended on 4 May 2026.

The law has been in court. In February 2026 the National Association of
Wholesaler-Distributors won what DEQ called
["a narrow injunction"](https://apps.oregon.gov/oregon-newsroom/OR/DEQ/Posts/Post/Court-dismisses-claims-against-Oregon-recycling-law)
pausing enforcement only against its own members; "The act remains in effect." After a
five-day bench trial in July, the court
[entered judgment for Oregon on 28 August 2026](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/70967634/national-association-of-wholesaler-distributors-v-feldon/),
finding the act violates neither the Dormant Commerce Clause nor due process. A separate
constitutional challenge,
[Lollicup USA v. Feldon](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73536101/lollicup-usa-inc-v-feldon/),
was filed in June 2026.

**California** (SB 54). CalRecycle's first attempt at regulations
[lapsed in March 2025](https://calrecycle.ca.gov/Laws/Rulemaking/SB54Regulations/) and had
to start over; the permanent regulations were approved on 1 May 2026. Circular Action
Alliance, the approved PRO,
[submitted its plan to the Advisory Board](https://calrecycle.ca.gov/packaging/packaging-epr/)
on 15 June 2026. That's the review step before CalRecycle decides whether to approve it.

The date that matters to an importer is in
[§42051](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PRC&sectionNum=42051):
"upon approval of a plan… or commencing January 1, 2027, whichever is sooner, a producer
shall not sell, offer for sale, import, or distribute covered materials in the state
unless the producer is approved to participate in the plan of a PRO…" The 2032 targets are
not all the same scope:

- **100% recyclable or compostable** applies to *all* covered material
  ([§42050](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PRC&sectionNum=42050)).
- **65% recycling** applies to *plastic* covered material (same section).
- **25% source reduction** applies to *plastic* covered material, with a first step of
  10% by 1 January 2027
  ([§42057](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PRC&sectionNum=42057)).

**Colorado** (HB 22-1355). CDPHE is blunt:
["As of July 2025, a producer cannot sell or distribute products that use covered materials in Colorado unless they participate in the Producer Responsibility Program."](https://cdphe.colorado.gov/hm/epr-program)
That comes straight from
[§25-17-708(1)](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2022a_1355_signed.pdf) of the
act. Dues were
[first owed in January 2026](https://cdphe.colorado.gov/hm/epr-program). CDPHE approved Circular Action Alliance's plan in
December 2025, and CAA
["must begin implementing this plan by the summer of 2026."](https://cdphe.colorado.gov/hm/epr-program)
The same trade association that sued Oregon
[filed a federal challenge in Colorado](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73693301/national-association-of-wholesaler-distributors-v-ryan/)
on 30 July 2026.

**Minnesota** (Packaging Waste and Cost Reduction Act, 2024). Four dates, all in the
statute:

- "After July 1, 2025, a producer must be a member of a producer responsibility
  organization" ([§115A.1448](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/115A.1448)).
- The PRO's first stewardship plan is due 1 October 2028
  ([§115A.1451](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/115A.1451)).
- After 1 January 2029, no producer may introduce covered materials without a written
  agreement to operate under an approved plan. After 1 January 2032, those materials have
  to be reusable, refillable, or on the state's recycling or composting collection lists
  (§115A.1448 again).

**Maine** (38 M.R.S. §2146, passed in July 2021). Maine's law is among the oldest, and
right now its program is the furthest behind. Producers register within 90 days of a
Stewardship Organization opening registration, but there is no Stewardship Organization.
DEP's procurement ended with a one-line notice:
["No Proposals Submitted as of Deadline. (August 20, 2026)"](https://www.maine.gov/dep/waste/recycle/epr.html)
The department says it "is currently reassessing the anticipated schedule." So there's no
live registration clock and no fee schedule yet.

**Maryland** (SB 901, 2025). The
[Packaging and Paper Products – Producer Responsibility Plans Act](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/SB0901?ys=2025RS)
was signed on 13 May 2025, and
[MDE](https://mde.maryland.gov/programs/land/WasteManagement/Pages/ProducerResponsibility.aspx)
names Circular Action Alliance as the approved PRO. Check MDE directly for current producer
deadlines.

**Washington** (SB 5284, 2025). The
[Recycling Reform Act](https://app.leg.wa.gov/billsummary?BillNumber=5284&Year=2025&Initiative=false)
took effect on 27 July 2025. Ecology says that
["starting in 2026"](https://ecology.wa.gov/waste-toxics/reducing-recycling-waste/our-recycling-programs/recycling-reform-act/producer-responsibility-organization)
producers must register with an approved PRO, which is Circular Action Alliance. Its
timeline adds the hard stop:
["After March 2029, producers who are not members of the PRO cannot sell their products in Washington."](https://ecology.wa.gov/waste-toxics/reducing-recycling-waste/our-recycling-programs/recycling-reform-act)

## What this means in practice

- **Don't assume one state's deadline tells you anything about another's.** Oregon,
  Colorado and Minnesota have required PRO membership since July 2025. California's
  participation requirement starts 1 January 2027 at the latest. Washington's sales bar
  arrives in 2029. Maine's clock hasn't started.
- **Work out whether you're the producer, state by state.** Four of these states fall back
  to the US importer; California falls back to whoever sells into the state. The same
  shipment can make you the producer in one and not the other.
- **Circular Action Alliance shows up almost everywhere.** It's the PRO in Oregon,
  Colorado, California, Maryland and Washington. One PRO relationship doesn't mean one set
  of rules, though. Each state keeps its own law.
- **A state with no operating program isn't a state with no future obligation.** Maine's
  law is on the books, and its program rule was
[amended as recently as March 2026](https://www.maine.gov/dep/waste/recycle/epr.html). Only
  the organization to run it is missing.
