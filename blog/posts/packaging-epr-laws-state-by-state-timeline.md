Five states now require the "producer" of packaged goods to pay for what happens to that
packaging after the customer throws it out. Treat this as one law with five names, and
you'll get at least one deadline wrong: the states aren't on the same schedule, and one of
them currently has no functioning program at all.

Checked against each state's own program page on **9 March 2027**.

## What "producer" actually means

Every state runs some version of the same hierarchy: the brand-owning manufacturer is the
producer, unless the brand is licensed, in which case it's the licensee, unless no US-based
entity holds that role at all — in which case the obligation falls to **the importer of
record**. Oregon's own guidance spells this out with a worked example naming Amazon and
L'Oréal directly. If your business imports private-label or unbranded goods with no US
brand owner behind them, read that sentence twice: the law may already be pointing at you.

## Where each state actually stands

**Oregon** — the Recycling Modernization Act has been live since July 2025: producers must
be Producer Responsibility Organization (PRO) members, paying annual fees, with the 25
largest producers also disclosing packaging lifecycle evaluations. A trade association sued
over the mandate and won a preliminary injunction in early 2026 — Oregon prevailed at trial
that August, and the program is operating.

**California** (SB 54) — permanent regulations were approved in May 2026, the PRO submitted
its plan in June, and full program compliance is due **1 January 2027**, ahead of the
statutory 2032 targets (100% recyclable or compostable packaging, a 65% recycling rate, 25%
source reduction). The regulatory process visibly slipped several months in early 2026
before landing here — this is a moving target, re-check before you file anything.

**Colorado** (HB 22-1355) — producers have been barred from selling covered materials
without program participation since **1 July 2025**, with full statewide rollout targeted
for 2026. Two trade associations have active 2026 lawsuits challenging compliance costs and
the mandatory PRO structure.

**Minnesota** (2024 Packaging Waste and Cost Reduction Act) — the newest and, so far, the
least contested. Producers must be PRO members by **1 July 2025**; the PRO's first
stewardship plan isn't due until October 2028, with all covered packaging required to be
recyclable, reusable, or compostable by **1 January 2032**. Minnesota's own agency
description of who counts as "producer" is notably thin — brand owner, manufacturer, or
importer, with more detailed guidance still pending.

**Maine** — the first state to pass a law like this, back in 2021, and right now the
furthest behind. Maine's Stewardship Organization procurement received **zero proposals**
by its August 2026 deadline. There is currently no Stewardship Organization, which means
there is no active producer registration deadline, no fee schedule, and no functioning
program — the state's own site describes it as "reassessing the anticipated schedule."

## What this means practically

- **Don't assume one state's deadline tells you anything about another's.** Minnesota is
  on its original 2024 schedule; Maine's program doesn't currently exist; that's the actual
  range you're dealing with.
- **Check whether you're the "producer" under each specific state's definition** — this is
  the detail that most often surprises an importer selling into multiple EPR states at
  once, since the fallback-to-importer rule triggers differently depending on whether a US
  brand owner exists in each transaction.
- **A state with no functioning program is not a state with no future obligation.** Maine's
  law is still on the books; only the implementing organization is missing. Don't read
  "currently inactive" as "safe to ignore."
