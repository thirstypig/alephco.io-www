People still ask whether de minimis is "going away." It already went. What's left to explain
is which government actions did it, and why the exemption isn't coming back the way a
paused rule would.

Every regulatory claim below is cited to the statute, the Federal Register, or CBP's own
site. Checked against the
Federal Register and the US Code on **10 September 2026**. If you're reading this much
later, check the links rather than the text.

## What de minimis used to mean

[Section 321 of the Tariff Act of 1930](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title19-section1321&num=0&edition=prelim)
let articles worth $800 or less, imported by one person on one day, enter free of duty and
import tax with a minimal customs process. The stated purpose was "to avoid expense and
inconvenience to the Government disproportionate to the amount of revenue that would
otherwise be collected." For low-value e-commerce — a $15 phone case, a $40 t-shirt — the
duty simply wasn't worth the paperwork of collecting it.

## Five actions, not one

| Effective | Action | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| 2 May 2025 | [Executive Order 14256](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/07/2025-06027/further-amendment-to-duties-addressing-the-synthetic-opioid-supply-chain-in-the-peoples-republic-of) | Ended de minimis for goods from China and Hong Kong |
| 29 Aug 2025 | [Executive Order 14324](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/05/2025-14897/suspending-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries) | Suspended it for every country |
| 24 Feb 2026 | [Executive Order 14388](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/25/2026-03829/continuing-the-suspension-of-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries) | Kept the suspension after the Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling |
| 24 Jun 2026 | CBP rules for [all modes except mail](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other) and [for mail](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12669/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-mail-shipments-and-new-postal-informal-entry) | Wrote the suspension into CBP's own regulations |
| 1 Jul 2027 | [One Big Beautiful Bill Act, §70531](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title19-section1321&num=0&edition=prelim) | Repeals the $800 exemption by statute |

**Executive Order 14324**, signed 30 July 2025, is the one most people know about:

> The duty-free de minimis exemption provided under 19 U.S.C. 1321(a)(2)(C) shall no longer
> apply to any shipment of articles not covered by 50 U.S.C. 1702(b), regardless of value,
> country of origin, mode of transportation, or method of entry.

It took effect 29 August 2025. [CBP's implementation notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/02/2025-16802/notice-of-implementation-of-the-presidents-executive-order-14324-suspending-duty-free-de-minimis),
published 2 September, required everything except mail to be entered in CBP's Automated
Commercial Environment by a party qualified to make entry. Mail wasn't let off duty. It
skipped the entry filing and paid a separate postal duty set by the order instead.

### The Supreme Court didn't bring it back

EO 14324 was issued under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), among
other authorities. On 20 February
2026 the Supreme Court held in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* that IEEPA "does not
authorize the President to impose additional tariffs." As
[CBP's June 2026 rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other)
records, that decision "did not address the suspension of the de minimis administrative
exemption." The same day,
[Executive Order 14388](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/25/2026-03829/continuing-the-suspension-of-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries)
continued the suspension, "including for shipments sent through the international postal
network."

So "the tariffs were struck down" does not mean "de minimis is back." It isn't.

### CBP's own regulations

On 24 June 2026 CBP stopped relying on the orders alone. Two interim final rules, issued
under CBP's own authority in Section 321, suspended the exemption indefinitely:

- **[19 CFR 10.151(b)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other)**
  for merchandise arriving by every mode other than mail, and
- **[19 CFR 145.31(b)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12669/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-mail-shipments-and-new-postal-informal-entry)**
  for merchandise arriving through the international postal network.

Both use the same test. The suspension lasts "until such time as CBP determines that the
application of the exemption is no longer inconsistent with the purpose of 19 U.S.C.
1321(a), no longer jeopardizes the revenue, and no longer facilitates unlawful
importations."

CBP says plainly that the first rule "does not, in practice, change the existing suspension."
What it changes is the legal footing. And CBP spelled out what happens if the orders go:
"Should the Executive Order be amended or removed prior to the statutory repeal of de
minimis, CBP's regulatory suspension would remain in effect."

### Congress had already acted

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed 4 July 2025, strikes the $800 exemption —
[19 U.S.C. 1321(a)(2)(C)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title19-section1321&num=0&edition=prelim)
— out of the statute, effective **1 July 2027**. The gift exemption ($100) and the
traveler exemption ($200) stay.

The same section added a civil penalty for using de minimis to bring in goods that break
other customs law: up to $5,000 for a first violation and up to $10,000 for each one after.
That penalty is repealed on the same date, along with the exemption.

Put together: if the executive orders were rescinded tomorrow, CBP's regulations would keep
the exemption suspended, and from 1 July 2027 the statute removes it outright.

⚠️ One place not to check:
[CBP's own Section 321 page](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/section-321-programs), last updated
7 August 2026, still describes the $800 exemption in the present tense, with no mention of
any of this. If you or your broker land there instead of the Federal Register, you'll read
the old rule as current.

## What this cost Shein and Temu

Shein and Temu didn't wait to find out. Both told customers in April 2025 that prices were
going up, ahead of the China-only suspension. Temu's notice said
"we will be making price adjustment starting April 25, 2025"
([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/04/25/business/shein-temu-price-increase/index.html)).

From 2 May, postal shipments from China faced a duty of
[120% of value or $100 per item](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/15/2025-06462/modifying-reciprocal-tariff-rates-to-reflect-trading-partner-retaliation-and-alignment).
The ad valorem rate was
[cut to 54% on 14 May](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/05/21/2025-09297/modifying-reciprocal-tariff-rates-to-reflect-discussions-with-the-peoples-republic-of-china),
with the $100 fee kept.

Demand moved fast. A panel analysis by
[Measure Protocol](https://www.measureprotocol.com/insights/shein-temu-shoppers-after-us-tariffs)
found Temu's US audience fell as much as 62% by May–June 2025, and Shein's as much as 47%
by September, against a February–April 2025 baseline. In April 2026 both were still roughly
30% below it.

That's the shape of the cost: not a line item on an invoice, but a demand curve that moves
the moment the exemption a business model was built on disappears.

## What this means practically

- **Model your landed cost as if de minimis doesn't exist**, because for commercial
  shipments it doesn't, by any mode.
- **Mail is not a workaround.** Postal shipments lost de minimis on 24 June 2026. They now
  need an entry: CBP's new postal informal entry, in force since 24 July 2026, or generally
  a formal entry for goods subject to additional duties, antidumping or countervailing
  duties, quotas, partner-agency requirements, or a trade-agreement claim
  ([91 FR 37801](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12669/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-mail-shipments-and-new-postal-informal-entry)).
- **If a vendor or broker cites "the $800 exemption" as current, ask which source they're
  reading.** The Federal Register and CBP's own program page currently disagree.
